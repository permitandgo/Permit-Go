Common Work Permit Application Mistakes

Work permit applications are common, but common does not mean simple. Many applicants run into stress because the file is rushed, documents are unclear, or important details are inconsistent.

One common mistake is waiting too long to organize documents. Work permit files may involve current status documents, passport copies, education records, employment-related materials, relationship documents, or other supporting evidence depending on the situation. If documents are collected at the last minute, missing items are easier to overlook.

Another mistake is mixing different permit categories. An open work permit, employer-specific work permit, spousal open work permit, and PGWP-related file may involve different supporting materials. Applicants should not assume that one checklist fits every situation.

A third issue is unclear file naming. Upload portals and digital folders become confusing when documents are named “scan1,” “newdoc,” or “finalfinal.pdf.” Clear naming helps applicants review their own file and reduces the chance of uploading the wrong item.

Inconsistent dates are also a problem. Work history, study dates, travel dates, status expiry dates, and passport validity should be checked carefully. Even small inconsistencies can create confusion and delay preparation.

Applicants sometimes rely too heavily on informal advice from friends, group chats, or social media. While lived experience can be helpful, every situation is different. Public instructions should always be reviewed directly.

Another preparation mistake is assuming that a document is strong simply because it exists. A document should be readable, relevant, complete, and properly labelled. Blurry screenshots or cropped records can weaken the clarity of a file.
